# Vision, mission and purpose

## Vision

Every evidence-synthesis search should be executable, inspectable, reproducible,
updateable and safely usable by humans and agents across information sources
without losing methodological intent or human accountability.

## Mission

Searchright turns a review protocol into portable, versioned contracts; compiles
those contracts into source-specific strategies; executes only authorised and
bounded sources; preserves complete evidence receipts; supports transparent
record, report and study selection; and generates standards-aware outputs from
an immutable audit trail.

## Purpose

The project exists to reduce avoidable search errors, hidden query translation
loss, reporting omissions, non-reproducible manual work and duplicated provider
code. It makes the boundary between methodological judgement, software
assistance and agent authority explicit.

## Long-term outcome

A mature open infrastructure layer—not another closed review application—that
can be embedded in research repositories, screening systems, librarian
workflows, guideline and HTA pipelines, MCP clients and research agents while
remaining independently auditable.

## Design commitments

- **Methodological intent survives translation.** Unsupported source features are
  warnings and review gates, never silent rewrites.
- **Humans retain consequential authority.** Agents may propose, explain and
  prioritise; final protocol amendments and exclusions remain policy-governed.
- **Provenance is a product output.** Receipts, amendments, lineage and research
  objects are generated during work, not reconstructed for publication.
- **The stable kernel is small.** Experimental providers, ranking and agent
  workflows sit behind capability and evidence boundaries.
- **Evidence controls claims.** Source completion, compilation, fixture proof,
  live-provider proof, external validation and public acceptance are distinct.
- **Accessibility and governance are architectural.** Diagnostics, retention,
  privacy, least privilege and institutional policy apply across every interface.
